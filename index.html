THE CRISIS

THEIR LIVES MATTER
THEIR SUFFERING MATTERS
THEIR FATE MUST NOT BE HIDDEN

As the world gathers in Türkiye for COP31, millions of community animals face an uncertain future under Law No. 7527

In July 2024, the Turkish Government changed its approach to street animal population management, moving away from the previous capture-neuter-vaccinate-return (CNVR) approach towards widespread collection and confinement.

Reports from across Türkiye describe dogs killed in horrific circumstances, appalling shelter conditions, mass graves and alleged cases of animals buried alive.

These are not faceless statistics. They are sentient beings who lived alongside people, were cared for and loved by local communities.

As Türkiye prepares to host COP31 in Antalya, the international community must confront the evidence and demand accountability.

SECTION 01

ROUNDED UP
TORTURED
KILLED

Reports from Türkiye raise serious concerns about capture operations, shelter conditions, animal deaths and alleged live burials

01

CAPTURE & ABDUCTION

Municipal teams reportedly used steel tongs, wire snares and tranquilizer darts to remove dogs from the communities where they were born and cared for.

Puppies, elderly dogs and healthy animals were reportedly placed in crowded vehicles, raising concerns about injuries and inhumane handling.

02

INSIDE MUNICIPAL SHELTERS

Reports describe overcrowded concrete pens, exposure to extreme weather, inadequate bedding and concerns about access to food and clean water.

Independent inspections and transparent veterinary records are urgently needed.

03

DISEASE & NEGLECT

Overcrowding and inadequate veterinary care can create serious animal welfare and public health risks.

Vaccination records, treatment protocols and mortality figures must be independently examined.

04

DEATHS & MASS GRAVES

Reports of mass graves and alleged cases of animals buried alive demand proper forensic and independent investigation.

Authorities must provide transparent information about animal deaths and the handling of remains.

THE INFRASTRUCTURE QUESTION

If the estimated population is 4 million and stated shelter capacity is 105,000, available capacity represents approximately 2.6% of that population. This raises urgent questions about implementation, animal welfare and the fate of dogs removed from the streets.
