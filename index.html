THE CRISIS

THEIR LIVES MATTER.
THEIR SUFFERING MATTERS.
THEIR FATE MUST NOT BE HIDDEN.

As the world gathers in Türkiye for COP31, millions of community animals face an uncertain future under Law No. 7527.

In July 2024, the Turkish Government fundamentally changed the country's approach to street animal population management. The campaign's position is that this shift abandoned the previous capture-neuter-vaccinate-return (CNVR) approach in favor of widespread collection and confinement.

Reports from across Türkiye describe dogs killed in horrific circumstances, appalling shelter conditions, mass graves and cases in which animals were allegedly buried alive. These reports demand independent scrutiny and transparent investigation.

These are not faceless statistics. They are sentient beings who lived alongside people, were cared for and loved by local communities, and deserve protection from cruelty.

As Türkiye prepares to host COP31 in Antalya, the international community must confront the available evidence and demand accountability.

A sustainable future cannot ignore animal welfare.

SECTION 01

ROUNDED UP.
TORTURED.
KILLED.

Campaign reports describe capture operations, severe shelter conditions, animal deaths and alleged live burials. These claims require transparent investigation and accountability.

01

CAPTURE & ABDUCTION

The campaign reports relentless sweeps in which municipal teams allegedly used heavy steel tongs, wire snares and tranquilizer darts to remove dogs from their communities.

Puppies, elderly dogs and healthy animals were reportedly placed in crowded transport vehicles, raising serious concerns about injuries, suffocation and humane handling.

02

INSIDE MUNICIPAL SHELTERS

Reports describe overcrowded concrete pens, exposure to extreme weather, inadequate bedding, and concerns about access to food and clean water.

The campaign calls for independent inspections, public shelter records, veterinary oversight and transparent reporting of animal outcomes.

03

DISEASE & NEGLECT

Overcrowding and inadequate veterinary care can create serious animal-welfare and public-health risks. Reports have raised concerns about contagious diseases and untreated illness.

Shelter conditions, vaccination records, treatment protocols and mortality figures should be independently examined.

04

DEATHS & MASS GRAVES

The campaign cites reports of mass graves and alleged cases in which animals were buried while still alive. These allegations require proper forensic and independent investigation.

Authorities should disclose investigation findings, burial-site records and information about the handling and disposal of animal remains.

The descriptions above reflect allegations and concerns raised by the campaign. Specific incidents should be supported by verifiable documentation and independently investigated.

THE INFRASTRUCTURE QUESTION

If the estimated population is 4 million and stated shelter capacity is 105,000, capacity represents approximately 2.6% of that population. The gap raises urgent questions about implementation, animal welfare and the fate of animals removed from the streets.

Figures shown are campaign-provided estimates and should be checked against current, independently verifiable population and shelter-capacity data. A capacity gap alone does not establish the fate of individual animals.
